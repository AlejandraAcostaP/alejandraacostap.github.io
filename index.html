Three positron facts I love

  • ≈ 110 psHow long a positron survives in perfect iron. In a vacancy it lives longer, and that is how we see damage.
  • 511 keVThe energy of each annihilation photon: exactly the electron's rest mass, E = mc².
  • 142 nsThe lifetime of ortho-positronium in vacuum, an "atom" made of an electron and its antiparticle.

Research

My MASc work, supervised by Prof. Peter Mascher in the Department of Engineering Physics, studies radiation damage in candidate structural materials for small modular reactors (SMRs) and Generation IV reactors. This research is funded by NSERC and the Canadian Nuclear Safety Commission (CNSC) through the Alliance CNSC-SMR program.

Candidate reactor materials

Selecting and screening candidate structural alloys for irradiation studies, with Geant4 and SRIM simulations of primary knock-on atoms, displacements and displacements per atom (dpa) alongside the measurements.

Positron annihilation spectroscopy

Measuring radiation-induced defects with Doppler broadening PAS and positron annihilation lifetime spectroscopy (PALS), analyzing the data in Python, and building our positron lifetime spectrometer.

In the lab

  • Variable-energy Doppler broadening PAS (slow positron beam)
  • Positron lifetime spectroscopy (PALS): building detector bases and sample holder
  • Na-22 positron source preparation
  • Helium ion implantation damage studies
  • Sample preparation: cutting, mounting and polishing
  • Scanning electron microscopy: authorized user at the Canadian Centre for Electron Microscopy
  • Radiation safety and radioactive materials training

Tools: Python (NumPy, pandas, Matplotlib), Geant4 (C++), SRIM, MATLAB, Mathematica, R, SQL, Git, Linux, AWS.

My path

I grew up in Soacha, Colombia, and went to a public school, Institución Educativa San Mateo, where I was student council president in my final year. I am the first in my family to go to university.

I started in sociology at Universidad Nacional de Colombia, then switched to physics, and learned English along the way. In 2023 a Mitacs Globalink internship brought me to McMaster for the first time, to learn positron annihilation spectroscopy.

In 2025 I came back as an ELAP scholar with my own proposal: use positrons to study radiation damage in reactor steels. That project opened a new research line in our group, became my undergraduate thesis, and took me to NuMat 2026 in Halifax. Today I continue that work as a MASc student.

Play: find the damage

This is how I find radiation damage in the lab. Grab the positron gun: press on the sample, pull back (drag to the left) to charge it, move up or down to aim, and release to fire. The further you pull, the higher the beam energy and the deeper the positrons go. Positrons that stop inside the damaged region get trapped in its vacancies and light up. From level 4 the damage is hidden, just like in a real sample.

Level 1/5 Shots left 3 Score 0/500

Press on the sample, pull back to charge, aim at the orange region and release.

Real physics: positrons from a beam of energy E stop at a mean depth z̄ = (40/ρ)·E1.6 nm (Makhov profile, ρ = 7.9 g/cm³ for steel), with a broad spread around it. That is why no shot can trap 100 %: your score compares each shot with the best energy possible.
